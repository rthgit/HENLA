## Abstract
We present HENLA, a symbolic-experiential cognitive architecture centered on episodic memory, viability signals, prediction error, evidence-based hypergraph promotion, deliberative scratchpads, specialized subgraphs, and structured claim verification. This report documents the completion of the internal construction phase and defines a rigorous external validation protocol (EXT) for blind, adversarial, non-author-controlled evaluation.

## 3. Core Architectural Claims (Internal Evidence Only)
HENLA has demonstrated the following capabilities within its controlled environment:
- **Traceable Memory**: Ability to link internal beliefs to specific log episodes.
- **Epistemic Classification**: Logic for distinguishing between facts, opinions, and hypotheses in ingested text.
- **Structural Resilience**: Engineering discipline in maintaining state across cascading failures.
- **Coherent Scaling**: A modular design that allows for domain-specific rules and federated nodes.

## 4. Claim Boundaries: What has NOT been proven
- **General Intelligence**: HENLA is not an AGI. Its "reasoning" is a product of its specific symbolic-experiential implementation.
- **Universal Knowledge**: While the architecture for "Civilization Knowledge" exists, it has only been tested on small-scale, internal datasets.
- **Independent Autonomy**: The system still requires significant human calibration and oversight for real-world deployment.
- **Benchmark Generalization**: Success in internal benchmarks may not translate to success in unseen, high-complexity external environments.

## 5. Transition Statement: Leaving the Laboratory
The successful passage of the KS-Suite (Knowledge Civilization Scaling) marks the end of the project's development as an isolated prototype. Every module has been implemented, and every internal gate has been passed. This signifies that the architecture is **complete as a platform**, but its objective intelligence and scalability must now be measured against the world.

The project mission has shifted from "passing the suite" to "withstanding external evaluation."

## 6. Next Steps: External Validation Protocol (EXT)
Future work will focus on:
- **Freeze**: Locking the current core to prevent "teaching for the test."
- **Blind Benchmarking**: Testing against repositories and tasks not authored by the project team.
- **Peer Review**: Opening the architecture and episode logs for external critique.
- **Baseline Comparison**: Rigorous side-by-side testing against existing LLM-agent frameworks.
